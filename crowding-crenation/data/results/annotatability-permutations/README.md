# Annotatability aggregation permutations

Does any way of collapsing a slide's ~324 per-FOV crowding scores into one number track the
catalog's annotatability judgement better than the **mean** does?

**No.** 53 alternatives were tested on 270 Tanzania slides. Not one is stronger than the mean by a
margin the data can resolve, and the four dispersion statistics (range, stdev, MAD, IQR) are
*significantly weaker* — they carry essentially no annotatability signal at all. The ceiling is
rho ≈ **−0.27** regardless of how the aggregation is done.

Generated by `scripts/tanzania-complete-081426/annotatability-permutations/build_permutations.py`
and `plot_permutations.py`.

## Why this was asked

Slide-level crowding had only ever been compared to annotatability one way: the mean of a slide's
per-FOV combined scores (`combined_score` in `../tanzania-complete-081426/slide-summary.csv`,
plotted by `plot_annotatability.py`). The mean discards everything about the *shape* of a slide's
FOV distribution — how spread out it is, whether a few fields are much worse than the rest — and
nothing on record established that it was the right summary. If annotatability were driven by a
slide's worst fields, an upper-tail statistic should beat it.

## Method

- **Cohort.** The 271 catalog slides from the complete Tanzania run, minus the v2.2 calibration
  slides, leaving **270**. Note this excludes exactly one slide, not two: `KTR-72502946` is not in
  the catalog and never had an ANNOTATABLE value, so it was never in this comparison. `KTR-72502948`
  is the one actually dropped.
- **Per-FOV score.** `density_score + overlap_score` (range 0–2), the same sum convention
  `aggregate_slides.py` uses at slide level.
- **Gated FOVs contribute 0.0.** A gated FOV is a near-empty field where the composite is answering
  the wrong question, so the headline convention reads it as zero severity. This is what makes the
  `mean` row reproduce `combined_score` exactly — asserted per-slide by the build script, as its
  check that this analysis has not drifted from the aggregator.
- **Correlation.** Spearman rho against the 1–4 ANNOTATABLE rank (ordinal, so rank correlation);
  Pearson r is in the CSV for reference.
- **Uncertainty.** Bootstrap over slides, 2000 resamples, fixed seed. Both a CI on each rho **and**
  a paired CI on `|rho| − |rho_mean|` — see below for why the paired one is the only comparison that
  can answer the question.
- **Multiplicity.** A permutation test on the largest |rho| across all 53 statistics: shuffle the
  annotatability ranks, recompute all 53, take the max. `p = 0.0005`, so the crowding–annotatability
  association is real; but this tests only that *something* is associated, not that the nominal
  winner is better than the mean.

### The four families

| family | n | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| whole-slide | 6 | mean, median, range, stdev, MAD, IQR |
| percentile | 20 | p5 … p100 — the quantile *value*: a slide's p80 is the score at or above 80% of that slide's own FOV scores |
| fraction | 8 | fraction of a slide's FOVs at or above each of the 4 density and 4 Rouleaux bucket cuts |
| trimmed-mean | 19 | mean of the highest-scoring top X% of FOVs, X = 95 … 5 (X=100 is the mean itself) |

Variance was omitted deliberately: it is a monotone transform of stdev, so its Spearman rho is
identical to 4 decimals. It would be a second row for the same test.

## Why the paired difference is the test that matters

Each statistic's own 95% CI is about **0.23 wide** (the mean's is [−0.385, −0.156]). The entire
spread between the best and worst of the 53 statistics is about 0.11 — *half* the width of any one
CI. So comparing two marginal CIs cannot distinguish anything here, and a table sorted by rho
invites reading sampling noise as a ranking.

But all 53 statistics are computed on the same 270 slides and are near-duplicates of each other, so
rho and rho_mean move together under resampling. The CI on their **difference** is an order of
magnitude tighter than either marginal CI (±0.01 for the closest statistics), and that is what
makes a real verdict possible. It is the x-axis of `plots/permutations-summary.png`.

## What the results mean

**Centre, not spread.** Everything that measures where a slide's distribution *sits* (mean, median,
any percentile, any trimmed mean, any threshold fraction) lands between −0.17 and −0.28. Everything
that measures how *wide* it is lands between −0.03 and −0.06. How much a slide's FOVs vary among
themselves says nothing about whether the slide can be annotated; only the overall level does.

**Nothing beats the mean.** The nominal winner, `frac_density_ge_monolayer` at −0.283, is +0.011
above the mean with a paired CI of [−0.021, +0.039] — comfortably straddling zero. It is also the
best of 53 tries, which is exactly the situation where a small nominal lead is expected from noise
alone. The mean, median, p50 and p60 are interchangeable to three decimals.

**The worst fields are not what makes a slide hard.** The trimmed-mean family is the informative
one, because it declines *monotonically*: −0.271 at top-95% down to −0.250 at top-5%, with no local
optimum anywhere (`plots/permutations-sweep-curves.png`, right panel). If annotatability were driven
by a slide's most crowded fields, trimming the easy ones away would sharpen the correlation. It does
the opposite, smoothly, all the way down. The shape is what you expect if the mean's signal is
limited by **sampling precision** — averaging more FOVs reduces noise — rather than by dilution from
easy fields. `p100` (the single worst FOV) is the weakest percentile at −0.216, which says the same
thing.

**The ceiling is low, and it is not the aggregation's fault.** rho ≈ −0.27 is about 7% of rank
variance. Since 53 different reductions of the same underlying measurement all hit the same ceiling,
the limit is in the measurement or the label, not in the arithmetic applied afterwards.

## Gated-FOV sensitivity

96 of the 270 slides have at least one gated FOV; 34 have more than 5%, 13 more than 25%, 3 more
than 50%. Recomputing everything with gated FOVs *dropped* instead of zeroed (last column above)
leaves most statistics within 0.005 — but it moves the low percentiles noticeably, and always in the
direction of a stronger correlation:

| statistic | gated → 0 | gated dropped |
|---|---|---|
| `p5` | −0.251 | **−0.290** |
| `p10` | −0.256 | **−0.295** |
| `p15` | −0.262 | **−0.294** |
| `p20` | −0.268 | **−0.293** |
| `p25` | −0.272 | **−0.290** |

This is the one place in the analysis where a variant edges past the mean's −0.273 on a point
estimate, and it has an obvious mechanism: zeroing gated FOVs plants a block of artificial 0.0
values at the bottom of a slide's distribution, which is precisely where the low percentiles read.
It is **not** presented as a finding — the gain is ~0.02, well inside the paired-difference noise,
and it was found by inspecting a sensitivity column rather than by a pre-registered test. It is
noted as the one thread worth pulling if this question is revisited.

## The site breakdown

`plots/crowding-by-site.png` splits the cohort by collection site (parsed from the slide ID prefix;
the `region` column cannot do this — all 271 slides carry the same value, `Tanzania / Kagera`).

| site | n | median crowding | within-site rho |
|---|---|---|---|
| KIT | 104 | 0.494 | −0.295 |
| KTR | 28 | 0.548 | −0.158 |
| NKR | 46 | 0.542 | −0.390 |
| RUB | 92 | 0.338 | −0.202 |

Two things the pooled analysis hides. RUB's median crowding is **0.338** against 0.49–0.55 for the
other three — a real difference in baseline crowding between sites. And within-site rho ranges from
−0.158 (KTR) to −0.390 (NKR) against −0.273 pooled.

So site structures both the predictor and, by composition, the pooled correlation — which makes it a
more promising lead than any aggregation choice. Caveat: KTR (n=28) and NKR (n=46) are small enough
that their individual rhos are unreliable, and the four-way spread is not itself tested here. Read
this as a direction to investigate, not a result.

## What this implies

**1. Keep the mean. Stop looking for a better statistic.** The deployed `combined_score` is already
the best available choice, and it is also the cheapest, the simplest to explain, and the one every
other figure in this project is built on. Nothing here justifies changing it, and the space of
"maybe a different summary statistic would work" is now measured rather than assumed.

**2. Crowding cannot serve as a standalone annotatability gate.** rho ≈ −0.27 is roughly 7% of rank
variance. The per-rank medians overlap heavily at every step (visible in the first panel of
`plots/permutations-whole-slide.png`): plenty of `hard` slides are less crowded than the median
`annotatable` slide and vice versa. Any threshold on crowding that catches most unannotatable slides
will also reject a large share of good ones. If slide triage is the goal, crowding is one input to a
multi-signal decision, not the decision.

**3. This is not a defect in the v2.2 composite.** The composite predicts what it was actually
calibrated against — manual density and Rouleaux labels — at cross-validated rho **0.78** and
**0.74** respectively. It is a good density/Rouleaux estimator that correlates weakly with
annotatability, which means **annotatability is a different construct**, not a target the composite
is failing at. The implication is directional: do *not* refit or re-weight v2.2 to chase
annotatability. That would trade away performance on its validated target for a few hundredths of a
correlation on a different one.

**4. The bottleneck is upstream of the arithmetic.** 53 different reductions of the same measurement
all hit the same ceiling. That localises the limit to either the measurement (crowding is genuinely
only a minor component of what makes a slide hard) or the label (ANNOTATABLE is noisy). This analysis
cannot distinguish those two, and that ambiguity is the single most valuable thing to resolve next —
because it decides whether more feature engineering is worth anything at all.

**5. Priority for the next round**, in order of expected information per unit effort:

1. **Get inter-rater data on ANNOTATABLE**, even for a 30–50 slide subset. Without knowing the
   label's own reliability there is no way to tell a weak predictor from a noisy target, and every
   downstream modelling decision depends on which it is. Cheap relative to what it settles.
2. **Test site as a confound** — stratify the correlation by site, or add site as a covariate. The
   spread from −0.158 to −0.390 across four sites is larger than the spread across all 53
   aggregations, so this is where the remaining structure appears to be.
3. **Try a multivariate combination** of crowding with the flag indicators, rather than more
   univariate variants. Note the prior is not encouraging — the halo and crop-outlier flags are
   individually uncorrelated with annotatability (+0.084, −0.080 in
   `../tanzania-complete-081426/README.md`) — but they are near-uncorrelated with crowding too, so
   they are at least independent information.
4. **Revisit the gated-FOV convention for low percentiles** (see above) — smallest of the four, and
   only worth doing if someone is already in this code.

## What this closes off, and what it does not

**Closed:** "try a different summary statistic." The mean is as good as any of 53 alternatives and
better than the dispersion ones. There is no reason to prefer median, a percentile, a trimmed mean,
or a threshold fraction, and no reason to keep searching that space on this cohort.

**Not closed:**

- A different *feature* rather than a different aggregation of the same feature. The crowding
  composite is one measurement; `n_flagged_overexposure` and `n_flagged_crop_outlier` already sit
  beside it as separate indicators in `plot_annotatability.py`.
- Site as a confound or effect modifier (above).
- The gated-FOV handling for low percentiles (above).
- Multivariate combinations. Everything here is a single statistic correlated on its own; no
  regression on several at once was attempted.

## Caveats

- 53 statistics on one cohort. The max-|rho| permutation p = 0.0005 establishes that crowding and
  annotatability are associated; it does not license reading the sorted table as a ranking.
- ANNOTATABLE is a single ordinal judgement per slide with no inter-rater data available, so the
  achievable rho is bounded by an unmeasured amount of label noise. A ceiling of −0.27 across 53
  reductions is consistent with a noisy label as much as with a weak predictor, and this analysis
  cannot separate those.
- The four ranks are unbalanced (105 / 57 / 12 / 96), and rank 3 "annotatable (hard)" has only 12
  slides — visible as the sparse third column in every strip panel.
- Bootstrap and permutation seeds are fixed, so re-running reproduces the CSVs byte-for-byte. A diff
  means an actual change in the inputs.
